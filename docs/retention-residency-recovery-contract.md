# Retention, residency and recovery contract

**Status:** Accepted  
**Decision:** D07  
**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Applies to:** Solo host storage, Team workspace storage, D1, Durable Objects, R2, queues, logs, exports, deletion and restore

This is the product's default lifecycle contract, not a claim of regulatory compliance. A customer-facing residency or retention promise ships only after the corresponding Cloudflare account features and operational deletion checks pass in that environment.

## 1. Retention schedule

| Data class | Active retention | Removal and recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Team messages, threads, reactions, drafts and agent content | Until an authorized user deletes it or the workspace is deleted | A delete immediately removes content from reads/search and leaves a non-content tombstone where conversation integrity needs it. Operational backups expire within 30 days. |
| Solo channel content, local vault ciphertext and agent content | Until the user deletes it from the designated host or a user-held backup | Lepidy has no server copy and cannot restore it. Local cleanup and backup retention are controlled on that device. |
| Attachments | Until attachment/message/workspace deletion or quota cleanup explicitly chosen by the user | Access is revoked in the delete transaction; the object is deleted asynchronously within 24 hours and backup copies expire within 30 days. |
| Channel, member, agent, routing and entitlement metadata | While the workspace exists | Removed in the workspace purge. Minimal deletion receipts retain ids/hashes only for 30 days to make retries safe. |
| Realtime replay events | 7 days | Expiry causes clients to refetch current authorized state. Content-bearing Solo frames are never retained. |
| Idempotency results | 24 hours unless the underlying operation requires a longer receipt | Expired rows may be swept; durable business records still prevent semantic duplication. |
| Request nonces and short authorization challenges | 10 minutes after expiry | Purged by the next scheduler sweep. |
| Completed outbox entries | 24 hours | Failed/dead-letter entries retain redacted diagnostics for 30 days. Payloads obey the shorter retention of their source data. |
| Approval request payloads | 30 days | The decision's metadata-only audit record remains for 365 days. Credential values are never present. |
| Security and vault audit metadata | 365 days | Hash-chained archive segments may move to the workspace's R2 jurisdiction; values, message excerpts and vault plaintext are forbidden. |
| Revoked sessions, devices, delegations and runner registrations | 30 days in operational tables; security event for 365 days | They remain revoked across restore because authority tables are rebuilt from current control state. |
| Redacted application logs | 30 days | No content, tokens, credential ciphertext, paths or local launch configuration. |
| Aggregated cost/reliability metrics | 13 months | Tenant/day aggregates only; no message, file, prompt, secret or personal content. |

Billing, tax, fraud, dispute and legal-hold records follow a separately published legal schedule. They are isolated from workspace content and are not silently shortened by this product default.

## 2. Workspace deletion

Deleting a workspace requires current owner authority and a user-verified step-up. The workspace becomes inaccessible immediately and enters a seven-day cancellation window. A second explicit **Purge now** action may skip that window and is irreversible.

After the window, the purge workflow deletes Team Durable Object state, R2 live objects and archives, workspace-scoped control rows, queued work and search indexes. It records each subsystem checkpoint and retries idempotently. Live stores must finish within 24 hours; provider backup expiry may take up to 30 additional days. The final receipt contains only workspace id, deletion request id, timestamps, region, counts and verification hashes, and expires after 30 days.

Solo purge removes cloud metadata and routing. The host receives a local-delete request, but cloud completion does not claim that an offline device or user-created backup was erased. The UI states this limitation before deletion.

## 3. Export

An owner may request a portable, checksummed export. Team exports are assembled as versioned JSONL manifests plus original attachments, encrypted to a one-time key generated by the requesting trusted client. Solo exports are built entirely by the host. The cloud never adds plaintext vault values or a server-decryptable vault root; vault material is exported only as ciphertext and client-held wraps.

Exports are resumable by stable chunk id and final manifest hash. Temporary server-side Team export chunks expire after 24 hours whether or not downloaded. Every record carries immutable ids and timestamps so an import can preserve attribution without waking agents or producing notifications.

## 4. Backup and restore

Team workspace storage uses the provider's rolling 30-day point-in-time recovery capability plus daily encrypted manifest checks. Longer application-managed backups are outside the launch default and require a separately priced retention policy. Solo recovery uses a user-held, checksummed local export; Lepidy cannot reconstruct absent local content.

A restore always targets a quarantined replacement workspace, verifies schema, row counts, attachment hashes and audit-chain continuity, and then changes routing with a new routing epoch. It restores content and durable product settings. It never restores live sessions, devices, runner leases, delegations, grants, approval decisions, OAuth tokens, idempotency/nonces or pending external actions. Current D1 membership and entitlement are projected fresh before access opens. Restoring deleted vault ciphertext requires an explicit owner acknowledgement and still does not restore revoked custodians or grants.

## 5. Residency profiles

Residency is selected when a workspace is created and cannot be changed by relabeling an existing database. The initial profiles are `default` and `eu`.

An `eu` workspace requires all workspace content and durable metadata stores to use jurisdiction restrictions: an EU Durable Object namespace, EU D1 control shard and EU R2 buckets. Traffic processing and operational metadata require Regional Services and Customer Metadata Boundary where the commercial environment includes them. Global routing may retain only opaque workspace/account identifiers, residency code and service-routing state; no channel names, messages, files, audit payloads, credential metadata or local configuration may enter it.

Workers can be invoked globally even when their storage is jurisdiction-bound, so storage jurisdiction alone is not marketed as complete EU processing residency. Logs, analytics, support exports, queues, email/push vendors, identity providers and billing subprocessors must each be certified or explicitly excluded from the claim. Location hints are never treated as jurisdiction guarantees.

Cloudflare documents jurisdiction-restricted [Durable Objects](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/), [D1 databases](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/configuration/data-location/) and [R2 buckets](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/). Provisioning records the immutable provider resource ids and verifies their jurisdictions before onboarding completes.

## 6. Audit chain and archive search

Audit entries are append-only and hash `(workspace, sequence, timestamp, event type, actor/provenance ids, redacted metadata, previous hash)`. A daily signed manifest anchors the first/last sequence and chain hash in the same residency profile. Verification runs during export, restore and the daily scheduler; a mismatch quarantines the affected archive and alerts operations without discarding evidence.

Team message bodies older than twelve months may move from hot SQLite to compressed, encrypted R2 JSONL segments when storage pressure requires it. SQLite keeps the message id, channel/thread coordinates, author/timestamps, archive object/version, byte range, body hash and authorized FTS terms. Search applies channel visibility before returning a hit; opening it fetches the exact segment range and verifies the body hash. Editing or deleting archived content creates a new segment generation and retires the prior object under the same 30-day backup ceiling. Solo uses the same logical format locally and never uploads an archive merely because it aged.

## 7. Required automated scenarios

1. Each expiry class is swept at its boundary with a controlled clock; early rows remain and expired rows disappear.
2. Message/file deletion immediately removes reads, search and signed access while preserving only allowed tombstones and receipts.
3. Workspace purge resumes after interruption, is idempotent, touches every store and verifies no content canary remains.
4. Solo deletion makes no false claim about an offline host or user backup.
5. Export resumes by chunk, verifies its final checksum, expires temporary chunks and contains no plaintext vault material.
6. Restore uses a replacement authority/routing epoch and does not resurrect any session, device, runner lease, delegation, grant, approval or external action.
7. EU provisioning rejects any default-jurisdiction DO, D1 or R2 binding and leak tests scan global routing/log fixtures for regional content.
8. Audit tampering breaks chain verification and quarantines only the affected workspace/archive.
9. Archived search enforces private-channel visibility before fetching, verifies the body hash and honors edits/deletes through segment generations.
10. Solo and Team retention behavior is exercised independently, including a lost Solo host with and without a valid user-held backup.
